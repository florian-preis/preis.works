For my 32nd birthday, Ellen and I flew to Geneva, picked up a rental car, and drove into Burgundy. We arrived late at the Alfred Hotel in Beaune, an old building with wooden beams and furniture made by a local design studio, and found a bottle of Pinot Noir waiting on the bed with a corkscrew we got to keep. Tired or not, we went out for a short walk to get our bearings.

The next morning we rented bikes and rode through the Côte de Beaune. It was cold, but the sky was blue and every vineyard had turned golden. We stopped in Volnay and then Meursault, where at a wine bar called Terre I tried Aligoté for the first time, the region's other white grape besides Chardonnay. On the way back we tasted six wines from Clos Marey-Monge at Château de Pommard, the largest monopole in Burgundy. An American from Georgia joined our tasting and spent most of it telling us about the expensive wines he had drunk and what his first class ticket had cost.

That evening was my early birthday dinner at Clos du Cèdre. The highlight was oeufs en meurette, two poached eggs in a Pinot Noir reduction, followed by one rich Burgundian dish after another and very kind waiters. Back at the hotel, we had a small birthday moment at midnight.

On the day itself we wandered the market for sausages and other delicacies, then went to the Fallot mustard mill, where we learned the history of mustard and crushed seeds to make our own. Ellen's parents' present was a pick of the shop, and I picked a lot. In Dijon, over a few glasses in a wine shop, Ellen gave me her present and read me the annual birthday poem. The old streets were beautiful and, like Beaune, the whole place breathes wine. Dinner was at the counter of O Bao, not very Burgundian, but a nice, casual end to the day.

Just before we fell asleep, Ellen realised the next day's wine tour left from Beaune, not Dijon. So instead of a slow morning, we caught the train back for three domaines and 18 wines. I liked them a bit less than the first day's, but at the last domaine we met a Lagotto Romagnolo, the same breed as Brina, the dog at our agriturismo near Siena this summer. We really liked the character of both dogs. Back in Dijon, we finished with a charcuterie board at La Cave Se Rebiffe.

On the last morning, a salesman at Fromages & Co talked us through the local wines, and the second part of Ellen's present was a Premier Cru. Between the wine, cheese, charcuterie, and mustard, the suitcase was almost at the weight limit. We drove back through the golden vineyards, stopped in a little village in the Jura where everything was closed, and flew home.

It was the perfect trip at the perfect time of year. The harvest had just finished, there were fewer people around, and the weather was still beautiful.
